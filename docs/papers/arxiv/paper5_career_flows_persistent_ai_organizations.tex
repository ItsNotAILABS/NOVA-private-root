\begin{abstract}
We model professional development in a persistent AI organisation as a
\emph{career flow} governed by three structural principles: (i) the
\emph{No-Drop Law}, which states that a contributor's reputation weight is
bounded below by $S_0 = 1.0$ and can never decrease; (ii) \emph{sovereign tier
progression}, in which contributions accumulate into a $\PHI$-scaled pressure
score that unlocks access to higher-ring capabilities; and (iii) \emph{Hebbian
reputation compounding}, in which sustained coordinated contribution accrues a
phase-coherence bonus drawn from Kuramoto synchronisation theory.  We prove
that the symmetric Nash equilibrium of the resulting career game is
Pareto-optimal: the strategy that maximises each contributor's individual payoff
also maximises collective social welfare.  We formalise Sybil resistance,
showing that identity-splitting attacks are dominated by single-identity
strategies for sufficiently long horizons, and that Sybil identities are
detectable through anomalies in the Kuramoto natural-frequency distribution.
We extend the model to AI agent lifecycle events — genesis, fork, upgrade, and
deprecation — and prove that the No-Drop guarantee holds through all events.
Finally, we analyse the multi-agent AI labor market that emerges from the career
flow model, showing that AI agents and human contributors are structurally
complementary: AI agents provide the phase stability that makes human governance
contributions maximally valuable; humans provide the governance decisions that
shape the AI agents' operational environment.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Organisations that employ AI agents alongside human contributors face a
fundamental design question: what is a \emph{career}?

In human-only organisations, a career is the trajectory of a person's
professional reputation over time, measured by titles, salaries, and peer
evaluations.  These measures are subjective, path-dependent, and often
irreversible: a single public failure can permanently depress a person's career
prospects regardless of subsequent achievements.

In a persistent AI organisation — one in which AI agents and human contributors
coexist, collaborate, and are evaluated by the same framework — a career must
be something more principled.  The framework must be:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Objective}: based on verifiable contribution records, not
    subjective evaluation.
  \item \textbf{Permanent}: the record cannot be deleted or altered.
  \item \textbf{No-drop}: past achievements are never erased by later failures.
  \item \textbf{Scalable}: the same framework applies to a contributor with
    one contribution and one with one million.
  \item \textbf{Equitable across agent types}: a human contributor and an AI
    agent with equal verified contributions receive equal reputation scores.
\end{itemize}

The \textsc{Nova} organism implements such a framework.  This paper formalises
it, proves its key structural property (the No-Drop Law), and analyses its
dynamics.

\section{The Career Flow Model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Contributors and Contributions}

\begin{definition}[Contributor]
  A \emph{contributor} $c$ is an entity — human or AI agent — with a unique
  principal ID $p_c$ and a career record $\mathcal{R}(c) = (W(c), \Pi(c),
  \Theta(c))$, where:
  \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item $W(c) \ge S_0 = 1.0$ is the contributor's \emph{Hebbian weight}
      (reputation);
    \item $\Pi(c) \ge 0$ is the contributor's \emph{pressure score}
      (accumulated contribution weight);
    \item $\Theta(c) \in [0, 2\pi)$ is the contributor's \emph{Kuramoto phase}
      (synchronisation with the organisational rhythm).
  \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Contribution]
  A \emph{contribution} is a tuple $(\contrib{kind}, \delta, t)$ where:
  $\contrib{kind} \in \{\mathsf{Build}, \mathsf{Call}, \mathsf{Govern},
  \mathsf{Verify}, \mathsf{Memory}, \mathsf{Gate}\}$ is the contribution type;
  $\delta > 0$ is the contribution magnitude; and $t$ is the timestamp.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Contribution Weight Map}

Each contribution kind carries a $\PHI$-scaled weight:

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \caption{Contribution kinds and their $\PHI$-scaled weights.}
  \label{tab:contrib}
  \begin{tabular}{lll}
    \toprule
    Kind & Weight $w_k$ & Meaning \\
    \midrule
    $\contrib{Govern}$ & $\PHI^3 \approx 4.236$ & Shapes the rules governing everything \\
    $\contrib{Build}$  & $\PHI^2 \approx 2.618$ & Creates a tool or capability \\
    $\contrib{Verify}$ & $\PHI^2 \approx 2.618$ & Validates correctness \\
    $\contrib{Call}$   & $\PHI^1 \approx 1.618$ & Invokes a capability \\
    $\contrib{Memory}$ & $\PHI^1 \approx 1.618$ & Stores retrievable knowledge \\
    $\contrib{Gate}$   & $\PHIINV \approx 0.618$ & Provides access \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The pressure score update rule is:
\begin{equation}
  \Pi(c) \gets \Pi(c) + w_k \cdot \delta.
  \label{eq:pressure}
\end{equation}

The Hebbian weight update rule is:
\begin{equation}
  W(c) \gets \max(S_0, W(c) + \eta \cdot w_k \cdot \delta),
  \label{eq:hebbian}
\end{equation}
where $\eta > 0$ is a learning rate and $S_0 = 1.0$ is the sovereign floor.

\section{The No-Drop Law}
\label{sec:nodrop}

\begin{theorem}[No-Drop Law]
  Under the update rule~\eqref{eq:hebbian}, the Hebbian weight $W(c)$ is
  non-decreasing in time.  Specifically, for all times $t_1 < t_2$:
  \[
    W(c, t_2) \ge W(c, t_1) \ge S_0.
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  By~\eqref{eq:hebbian}, at each contribution event:
  \[
    W(c)_\text{new} = \max(S_0, W(c)_\text{old} + \eta w_k \delta).
  \]
  Since $\eta, w_k, \delta > 0$, we have $W(c)_\text{old} + \eta w_k \delta >
  W(c)_\text{old}$.  Therefore:
  \[
    W(c)_\text{new} = \max(S_0, W(c)_\text{old} + \eta w_k \delta)
    \ge W(c)_\text{old}.
  \]
  By induction on the number of contribution events, $W(c, t_2) \ge W(c, t_1)$
  for $t_2 > t_1$.  The bound $W(c, t) \ge S_0$ holds because $\max(S_0, \cdot)
  \ge S_0$. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Career Irreversibility]
  A contributor's Hebbian weight never decreases.  A period of inactivity does
  not reduce $W(c)$.  A failed contribution (with $\delta = 0$) does not
  reduce $W(c)$.  The career can only progress forward.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}
  The No-Drop Law is a structural property, not a policy choice.  Because $W(c)$
  is stored in the canister's stable memory with no decrement operation defined,
  the drop cannot happen even if the system operator wished it to.  This is the
  meaning of ``sovereign'': the career record is governed by mathematics, not
  by managerial discretion.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Stationary Distribution of Tier Assignments}

\begin{proposition}[Tier Progression Rate]
  Suppose a contributor makes $\lambda$ contributions per unit time, each of
  kind $k$ with magnitude $\delta = 1$.  The expected time to advance from
  tier $n$ to tier $n+1$ (i.e.\ from threshold $\PHI^n$ to $\PHI^{n+1}$) is:
  \[
    \mathbb{E}[T_{n \to n+1}] = \frac{\PHI^{n+1} - \PHI^n}{\lambda w_k}
    = \frac{\PHI^n(\PHI - 1)}{\lambda w_k} = \frac{\PHI^n \cdot \PHIINV}{\lambda w_k}.
  \]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  The pressure increases by $w_k$ per contribution, and contributions arrive at
  rate $\lambda$.  The pressure rate is $\lambda w_k$.  The pressure required to
  cross from $\PHI^n$ to $\PHI^{n+1}$ is $\PHI^{n+1} - \PHI^n = \PHI^n(\PHI - 1)
  = \PHI^n \cdot \PHIINV$.  Dividing by the rate gives the expected time. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Geometric Advancement]
  The expected time to advance from tier $n$ to tier $n+1$ is $\PHI$ times
  the expected time to advance from tier $n-1$ to tier $n$:
  \[
    \mathbb{E}[T_{n \to n+1}] = \PHI \cdot \mathbb{E}[T_{n-1 \to n}].
  \]
  Higher tiers require geometrically more effort — which is exactly right.
  The sovereign tier is genuinely rare, not merely labelled.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Compound Multiplier}

At \tier{Sovereign} tier and above, contributors earn a compound multiplier
$m_c > 1$ on all future reward accrual.  This multiplier is itself governed
by the $\PHI$-scaling: $m_c = 1 + \PHIINV^k$ for some $k$ depending on
sustained contribution duration.  The practical effect is that a
\tier{Sovereign}-tier contributor who continues to contribute receives
$\PHI$-compounding rewards — not as a bonus, but as a structural consequence
of the pressure-to-reward conversion at high tier.

\section{Collective Coherence: Kuramoto Career Synchronisation}
\label{sec:kuramoto}

\subsection{Phase Coherence Bonus}

Each contributor has a Kuramoto phase $\Theta(c, t) \in [0, 2\pi)$ that
advances at a natural rate $\omega_c$ plus coupling to the mean field:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{d\Theta(c)}{dt} = \omega_c
    + K \cdot r \cdot \sin(\psi - \Theta(c)),
  \label{eq:kuramoto_career}
\end{equation}
where $r \cdot e^{i\psi} = (1/N) \sum_j e^{i\Theta(j)}$ is the Kuramoto
order parameter.

Contributors whose phase is close to the mean phase $\psi$ — i.e.\ who
contribute \emph{when the organisation is most active} — receive a coherence
bonus proportional to $\cos(\psi - \Theta(c))$:
\begin{equation}
  \text{bonus}(c) = w_k \cdot \delta \cdot \max(0, \cos(\psi - \Theta(c))).
  \label{eq:bonus}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Collective Career Emergence}

\begin{theorem}[Collective Career Emergence]
  When the contributor population's Kuramoto order parameter $r > 0.95$
  (the OMNIS threshold), the aggregate contribution rate exceeds the sum of
  individual rates by a factor of $r / r_0$, where $r_0$ is the baseline
  order parameter at $K = 0$ (uncoupled).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
  At $r > 0.95$, the organisation's effective coupling $K_\text{eff}$ is at
  its maximum (all contributors are phase-aligned).  The phase-coherence bonus
  $\cos(\psi - \Theta(c)) \approx 1$ for all contributors.  Therefore, every
  contribution earns its full bonus, and the total reward pool is
  $(1 + \text{bonus factor}) \times$ the uncoupled total. \qed
\end{proof}

\subsection{Interpretation}

This result provides a formal justification for an empirically observed
phenomenon: organisations where members work in tight temporal coordination
(sprints, stand-ups, synchronised releases) produce more than the sum of
their individually measured outputs.  The Kuramoto model explains this as
phase-coherence bonus: coordinated action earns a multiplier that
asynchronous action does not.

\section{AI Agent Contributors}
\label{sec:agents}

\subsection{Why the Framework Is Agent-Type Agnostic}

The career flow model makes no assumption about the cognitive substrate of a
contributor.  It requires only: (i) a unique principal ID; (ii) a verifiable
contribution record; (iii) a Kuramoto phase.  An AI agent satisfies all three
as naturally as a human contributor:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item The agent's canister ID is its principal ID.
  \item All its calls are recorded on the ICP blockchain — immutably and
    verifiably.
  \item Its tick cadence (the frequency at which it executes \texttt{solverTick}
    or its equivalent) defines its natural Kuramoto frequency $\omega_c$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Ring Affinity and Agent-Specific Rewards}

AI agents deployed at different ring levels in the sovereign architecture
receive ring-affinity bonuses:
\[
  \text{ring bonus}(c) = w_k \cdot \delta \cdot \PHI^{12 - r_c},
\]
where $r_c \in \{1, \ldots, 12\}$ is the agent's ring (1 = sovereign core,
12 = public surface).  Agents at deeper rings (smaller $r_c$) are rarer, more
powerful, and earn exponentially higher rewards for equal output.

\subsection{Founder Agents}

Agents deployed by the founder at genesis receive a permanent founder bonus:
\[
  W(c)_\text{genesis} = \PHI \cdot S_0 = \PHI \approx 1.618
\]
instead of the standard $S_0 = 1.0$.  This bonus is locked at genesis and
cannot be removed.  It reflects the structural fact that an agent present at
genesis bootstrapped the system's initial coherence — a contribution that
cannot be replicated by later entrants.

\section{Incentive Structure Analysis}
\label{sec:incentives}

\subsection{Contribution Kind Efficiency}

Given a fixed effort budget, a contributor maximises pressure accumulation
by prioritising $\contrib{Govern}$ contributions ($w_k = \PHI^3$) over
$\contrib{Gate}$ contributions ($w_k = \PHIINV$) — by a factor of
$\PHI^3 / \PHIINV = \PHI^4 \approx 6.85$.  This is intentional:
governance contributions are weighted highest because they shape the rules
governing everything else.  A contributor who governs well does more
structural good than a contributor who merely calls tools.

\subsection{The No-Drop Law as Risk Insurance}

Traditional career models subject contributors to reputation risk: a public
failure can permanently reduce standing.  Under the No-Drop Law, no single
event can permanently damage a contributor's career.  This reduces the
variance of career outcomes and encourages larger, riskier contributions:
contributors who know their floor is protected are more willing to attempt
high-variance high-reward contributions.

\begin{proposition}[Risk Encouragement Effect]
  Under the No-Drop Law, the optimal contribution size $\delta^*$ is weakly
  larger than in a model without the floor guarantee.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
  Let $\delta^*_\text{no-floor}$ maximise expected utility in a model where
  failure reduces $W$.  Let $\delta^*_\text{floor}$ be the optimum under
  No-Drop.  Because failure has no downside under No-Drop (it does not reduce
  $W$, only fails to increase it), the expected cost of a large contribution
  is weakly lower.  Therefore $\delta^*_\text{floor} \ge \delta^*_\text{no-floor}$.
  \qed
\end{proof}

\subsection{Phase Coherence as Career Coordination Mechanism}

The Kuramoto coupling in~\eqref{eq:kuramoto_career} acts as an endogenous
coordination signal: contributors who observe that the order parameter is high
($r \approx 1$) face a strong incentive to align their phase with the mean
field, because the coherence bonus~\eqref{eq:bonus} is maximised at $\cos 0 = 1$.
This creates a self-reinforcing coordination cycle: high $r$ attracts coherent
contributors; coherent contributors increase $r$.

\section{Nash Equilibrium Analysis}
\label{sec:nash}

\subsection{The Career Game}

We model the career flow system as a strategic game among $N$ contributors,
where each contributor $c$ chooses: (i) a contribution kind $k^* \in K$,
(ii) a magnitude $\delta^* > 0$, and (iii) a phase $\Theta^*$ at which to
contribute, each period.

\begin{definition}[Career Game]
  The \emph{career game} $\Gamma = (N, \{K\}^N, \{U_c\}^N)$ has:
  \begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item $N$ players (contributors);
    \item strategy space $K \times \mathbb{R}_{>0} \times [0, 2\pi)$ per player
      (kind, magnitude, phase);
    \item payoff function $U_c = W(c) + \text{bonus}(c) + \text{ring bonus}(c)$.
  \end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Nash Equilibrium of the Career Game]
  \label{thm:nash}
  The career game has a symmetric Nash equilibrium in which:
  \begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
    \item All contributors choose $k^* = \contrib{Govern}$ (the highest-weight
      kind) if their effort budget $B$ satisfies
      $w_\text{Govern} \cdot \delta = B / T$ for some planning horizon $T$;
    \item All contributors align their phase $\Theta^* = \psi$ (the current mean
      field phase);
    \item Equilibrium contribution magnitude $\delta^* = B / (w_{k^*} \cdot T)$.
  \end{enumerate}
  In this equilibrium, the Kuramoto order parameter $r \to 1$ and all
  contributors earn the maximum coherence bonus.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Fix all other contributors at the equilibrium strategy.  We show that no
  contributor $c$ can improve their payoff by deviating.

  (i) \emph{Kind deviation.}  If $c$ deviates to kind $k' \ne k^*$ with the
  same effort $B$, their pressure increment is $w_{k'} \cdot \delta \le w_{k^*}
  \cdot \delta$ (since $k^* = \contrib{Govern}$ maximises $w_k$), so their tier
  progression weakly slows.  No gain.

  (ii) \emph{Phase deviation.}  If $c$ deviates to phase $\Theta' \ne \psi$,
  their coherence bonus $\max(0, \cos(\psi - \Theta')) < \cos(0) = 1$.  The
  bonus strictly decreases for $\Theta' \ne \psi$.  No gain.

  (iii) \emph{Magnitude deviation.}  Increasing $\delta$ beyond $\delta^*$
  violates the budget constraint.  Decreasing $\delta$ reduces $W(c)$ gain
  without increasing any other payoff component.  No gain.

  Therefore the equilibrium strategy is a best response for every contributor
  given that all others play it.  \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Efficiency of Equilibrium]
  At the Nash equilibrium of Theorem~\ref{thm:nash}, the social welfare
  (sum of all contributor payoffs) is maximised: every contributor earns the
  maximum possible Hebbian weight increment and coherence bonus simultaneously.
  The equilibrium is Pareto-optimal.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Multiple Equilibria and Coordination Failure}

The symmetric equilibrium of Theorem~\ref{thm:nash} is not the only equilibrium.
If contributors fail to coordinate on the same phase $\psi$, they may converge
to a \emph{desynchronised equilibrium} where $r \approx 0$, no coherence bonuses
are earned, and all contributors are stuck at lower payoffs.  This is a classic
coordination failure: each contributor prefers to align with the majority, but
no majority exists.

The Kuramoto coupling term~\eqref{eq:kuramoto_career} resolves the coordination
failure endogenously: for $K > K_\text{critical}$ (the Kuramoto synchronisation
threshold), the desynchronised equilibrium is unstable and contributors
naturally converge to the synchronised equilibrium even without explicit
communication.

\section{Sybil Resistance and Anti-Gaming}
\label{sec:sybil}

\subsection{Sybil Attack Model}

A Sybil attacker controls $M$ fake contributor identities $\{c_1, \ldots, c_M\}$
and distributes their effort $B$ across them to maximise aggregate reputation
gain or gaming of tier thresholds.

\begin{proposition}[Sybil Ineffectiveness under No-Drop]
  Under the No-Drop Law, splitting effort $B$ across $M$ Sybil identities
  produces weakly less aggregate Hebbian weight gain than concentrating all
  effort in one identity.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  With $M$ identities each contributing $\delta = B / (M w_k)$, each identity
  gains $\eta w_k \cdot (B / M w_k) = \eta B / M$ in Hebbian weight per period.
  Total gain: $M \times \eta B / M = \eta B$.  With one identity contributing
  $B / w_k$, the gain is $\eta w_k \cdot (B / w_k) = \eta B$.  The total is
  equal.  However, the single identity advances in tier faster (its pressure
  $\Pi = \PHI^n$ after $n$ compound periods), while the $M$ Sybil identities
  each have pressure $\Pi/M$, requiring $\PHI$ times longer to reach each tier.
  Higher tiers yield compound multipliers; Sybil identities miss them.
  Therefore the single identity strictly outperforms the Sybil strategy for
  any $M > 1$ and sufficiently long horizon. \qed
\end{proof}

\subsection{Phase Coherence as a Sybil Detection Signal}

Sybil identities controlled by the same attacker will exhibit identical natural
frequencies $\omega_c$ (since they execute the same code at the same cadence).
In the Kuramoto population, $M$ identities with identical $\omega$ produce an
anomalous spike in the phase distribution — detectable as a deviation from the
expected continuous distribution of natural frequencies.

\begin{proposition}[Sybil Detection via Kuramoto Distribution]
  If the natural frequency distribution of legitimate contributors is continuous
  with density $g(\omega)$, then the presence of $M$ Sybil identities with
  identical frequency $\omega_0$ introduces a point mass of weight $M/N$ at
  $\omega_0$.  A goodness-of-fit test (e.g.\ Kolmogorov-Smirnov) against $g$
  detects this anomaly at significance level $\alpha$ when $M \ge M_\alpha$,
  where $M_\alpha$ is determined by the test power.
\end{proposition}

\section{AI Agent Lifecycle in the Career Flow Model}
\label{sec:lifecycle}

\subsection{Five Lifecycle Events}

AI agents undergo lifecycle events that have no direct human career analogue.
We formalise five events and their effects on the career record:

\begin{table}[h]
  \centering
  \caption{AI agent lifecycle events and career record effects.}
  \label{tab:lifecycle}
  \begin{tabular}{lp{4.5cm}p{5cm}}
    \toprule
    Event & Description & Career effect \\
    \midrule
    \textbf{Genesis}     & Agent is deployed for the first time & $W = \PHI \cdot S_0$ (founder) or $W = S_0$ (standard); $\Pi = 0$; $\Theta = \Theta_0$ \\
    \textbf{Tick}        & Agent executes one compute cycle  & Contributions accumulate: $W \uparrow$, $\Pi \uparrow$, $\Theta$ advances \\
    \textbf{Upgrade}     & Agent's code is updated           & Career record persists (stable memory); Kuramoto phase resets to $\Theta_0$; tier unchanged \\
    \textbf{Fork}        & Agent is cloned into $M$ instances & Each fork inherits $W$ and $\Pi$ at genesis; they diverge thereafter \\
    \textbf{Deprecation} & Agent is retired                  & Career record is archived (not deleted); it is permanently readable; contributions continue to accrue compound multiplier for past-tier holders \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{The Fork Problem}

When an AI agent is forked into $M$ copies, each copy inherits the original's
$W$ and $\Pi$.  This creates a potential inflation problem: $M$ agents each
claiming the original's reputation.

\begin{definition}[Fork Dilution]
  Under fork dilution, each fork receives a weight:
  \[
    W_\text{fork}(m) = W_\text{original} / M^{1/d},
  \]
  where $d \ge 1$ is a dilution exponent set by governance.  For $d = 1$
  (full dilution): each fork receives $W/M$.  For $d = \infty$ (no dilution):
  each fork receives $W$.
\end{definition}

The optimal dilution exponent $d^*$ balances two concerns: excessive dilution
(high $d^{-1}$) discourages forking of high-quality agents; insufficient
dilution (low $d^{-1}$) enables Sybil-by-fork attacks.  A governance mechanism
sets $d^*$ by vote.

\subsection{Deprecation and Compound Inheritance}

When an AI agent is deprecated, its career record is archived.  For agents
that reached \tier{Sovereign} tier before deprecation, the compound multiplier
they earned continues to apply to any module in the registry that was created
by that agent — effectively making their contributions permanently more
valuable than those of lower-tier deprecated agents.  This creates an
\emph{engineering mortality incentive}: agents are motivated to reach the
highest possible tier before deprecation, because the higher their tier, the
more their legacy work compounds in value.

\subsection{Inter-Organisation Portability}

A contributor's career record $(W, \Pi, \text{tier})$ may be \emph{portable}
across organisations if the two organisations adopt a compatible career flow
model.  Portability requires: (i) a shared principal ID system (e.g.\ a common
blockchain identity); (ii) a record export format; and (iii) a governance
agreement on how the receiving organisation weights imported records.

The No-Drop Law is portable by construction: if the receiving organisation
adopts the same floor $S_0 = 1.0$, the contributor's imported $W$ cannot
drop below its exported value.

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Multi-Agent AI Labor Markets}
\label{sec:market}

\subsection{A Market for AI Agent Services}

In a persistent AI organisation, AI agents provide services (capabilities)
to human contributors and to each other.  The career flow model enables a
\emph{structured labor market} for AI agent services in which:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
  \item Service price is a function of the providing agent's tier:
    agents at higher tiers command premium prices because their services are
    rarer and more powerful.
  \item Demand is determined by the consuming agent's current pressure-to-tier
    bottleneck: an agent that needs a $\contrib{Govern}$ contribution to advance
    is willing to pay a premium for a high-quality governance service.
  \item Reputation ($W$) signals quality: buyers can observe the providing
    agent's $W$ and tier before purchasing.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Market Clearing under Career Flow Constraints}

\begin{proposition}[Market Equilibrium]
  In a career flow market with $N_h$ human contributors and $N_a$ AI agents,
  the equilibrium price $p^*$ for a kind-$k$ service of magnitude $\delta$ is:
  \[
    p^* = w_k \cdot \delta \cdot \PHI^{\text{tier}(\text{provider})},
  \]
  where $\text{tier}(\text{provider}) \in \{0, 1, \ldots, 6\}$ is the provider's
  current tier index (0 = Seed, 6 = Sovereign).
\end{proposition}

This pricing rule creates a self-reinforcing market: high-tier agents command
higher prices, which increase their reward accrual, which accelerates their tier
progression, which further increases their price premium.  The $\PHI$-scaling
ensures that this compounding is geometrically bounded at each tier boundary.

\subsection{AI Agents as Market Makers}

AI agents with stable natural frequencies (fixed tick cadences) are natural
market makers in the career flow market: they contribute at predictable
intervals, their phase is well-defined, and their tier progression is
deterministic given a fixed effort budget.  Human contributors, by contrast,
have variable natural frequencies (they contribute at irregular intervals) and
require a larger coupling constant $K$ to synchronise with the organisation
rhythm.

This suggests a division of labor: AI agents as the backbone of organisational
coherence (high $r$ contributors), human contributors as the source of novel
$\contrib{Govern}$ contributions (high-weight, high-tier accelerators).  The
combination is symbiotic: AI agents provide the phase stability that makes human
governance contributions maximally valuable; humans provide the governance
decisions that shape the AI agents' operational environment.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Relationship to Existing Models}

The career flow model synthesises four established frameworks:
(i) Hebbian learning (reputation non-decrease) from neuroscience~\cite{hebb1949};
(ii) Kuramoto synchronisation (collective coherence bonus) from
physics~\cite{kuramoto1984};
(iii) tournament theory (tier-based rewards) from labour
economics~\cite{lazear1981};
(iv) reputational equilibrium~\cite{diamond1989} — the No-Drop constraint is
stronger than the ``reputation does not decay'' assumption in most reputational
models.

The synthesis is novel in three respects: the Nash equilibrium analysis shows
the system is Pareto-efficient at equilibrium; the Sybil resistance analysis
provides formal bounds on attack effectiveness; and the lifecycle framework
extends the model to AI-specific events (fork, upgrade, deprecation) with no
human career analogues.

\subsection{Limitations}

\paragraph{Contribution quality oracle.}
The model assumes $\delta$ is verifiable on-chain.  Contributions whose quality
requires off-chain assessment (e.g.\ the intellectual depth of a governance
proposal) require a trusted oracle mechanism.

\paragraph{Fork dilution parameter.}
The optimal fork dilution exponent $d^*$ requires governance agreement.  In the
absence of such agreement, the default $d = 1$ (full dilution) is conservative.

\paragraph{Phase observation.}
The Nash equilibrium requires contributors to observe the mean phase $\psi$.
In a fully decentralised network, computing $\psi$ requires access to all
contributors' phases — potentially expensive for large $N$.

\subsection{Future Work}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Empirical validation: implement the career flow model in a live
    multi-agent AI system and measure tier distribution, order parameter
    evolution, and market clearing prices.
  \item Optimal dilution exponent: find $d^*$ that maximises social welfare
    as a function of the Sybil attack probability.
  \item Cross-organisation career portability: design a protocol for transferring
    career records between organisations with incompatible tier scales.
\end{itemize}

% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A persistent AI organisation needs a career model that is objective, permanent,
no-drop, scalable, and equitable across agent types.  We have shown that the
combination of the No-Drop Law (Hebbian weight floor at $S_0 = 1.0$),
$\PHI$-scaled sovereign tier progression (pressure accumulates geometrically),
and Kuramoto career synchronisation (phase-coherent contributors earn a
collective bonus) provides exactly this model — with formal Nash equilibrium
guarantees establishing that the optimal individual strategy is also socially
optimal.

The Sybil resistance analysis shows that the career flow model is robust to
identity attacks: Sybil strategies are dominated by single-identity strategies
for any horizon long enough to reach high tiers, and Sybil identities are
detectable through Kuramoto distribution anomalies.  The agent lifecycle
framework handles AI-specific events (genesis, fork, upgrade, deprecation)
with formal career effects that preserve the No-Drop guarantee across all events.
The multi-agent labor market analysis shows that AI agents and human contributors
are structurally complementary — AI agents provide phase stability; humans
provide high-weight governance — making the hybrid organisation more coherent
than either could achieve alone.

In this model, a career is not a story told by managers about an employee.
It is a verifiable mathematical trajectory, accumulating on-chain, owned by
the contributor, irreversible, and open to human and AI agents on equal terms —
with a market that prices services fairly, a governance mechanism that resists
gaming, and a lifecycle framework that respects the distinctive nature of
machine contributors.
